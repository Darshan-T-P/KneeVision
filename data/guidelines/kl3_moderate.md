# KL 3 (Moderate) — Escalated Conservative Management

KL grade 3 shows definite joint space narrowing, multiple moderate
osteophytes, some sclerosis, and possible bony deformity. Patients at this
stage typically report higher pain, stiffness, and functional limitation
(WOMAC scores) than KL 0-2, and conservative treatment is intensified while
continuing to defer surgery.

## Recommendations

- **Continue all core treatments from KL2** (structured exercise, weight
  management, self-management education, topical NSAIDs) — these remain
  first-line and should not be dropped as severity increases.
- **Assistive devices**: a cane (held in the opposite hand), walker, or
  knee brace can reduce joint load and improve mobility and confidence,
  particularly if there is any instability or a valgus/varus deformity
  contributing to uneven loading.
- **Intra-articular injections** (corticosteroid or hyaluronic acid) may be
  considered for symptom flares that do not respond adequately to exercise
  and topical treatment, with the choice dependent on comorbidities and
  patient preference — this is a Level 1B/2 treatment, not first-line.
- **Oral NSAIDs or other pharmacologic pain management** may be added if
  topical treatment and non-pharmacologic measures are insufficient,
  weighed against the patient's cardiovascular/GI/renal risk profile.
- **Reassess functional impact regularly.** If pain and function
  significantly limit daily activities despite optimized conservative
  treatment, begin discussing the possibility of eventual surgical referral
  so the patient can plan ahead, without necessarily acting on it yet.
- **Physical therapy referral** is appropriate if the patient has not
  already had supervised guidance, especially for gait and load management
  strategies specific to worsening structural changes.
