# KL 4 (Severe) — Advanced Management and Surgical Referral

KL grade 4 shows severe joint space narrowing (often near-complete), large
osteophytes, marked subchondral sclerosis, and definite bony deformity /
attrition. This is the stage at which surgical options become clinically
relevant, though conservative treatment is still part of the picture,
particularly for patients who are not surgical candidates or who wish to
delay surgery.

## Recommendations

- **Continue conservative treatment** (exercise, weight management,
  assistive devices, pain management) — it remains beneficial even at this
  stage and is typically continued alongside, not replaced by, surgical
  discussion.
- **Surgical referral (total or partial knee arthroplasty)** should be
  considered once conservative management has been adequately tried and the
  patient has significant pain and functional limitation. This is
  fundamentally a shared decision between the patient and an orthopaedic
  surgeon, factoring in pain severity, functional impact, comorbidities,
  and patient goals/preferences — not a decision this system can make.
- **Pre-surgical optimization** improves outcomes if arthroplasty is being
  considered: weight management, smoking cessation if applicable, and
  maintaining/improving quadriceps strength before surgery ("pre-hab") are
  associated with better post-surgical recovery.
- **Pain management** may need to be more actively managed while awaiting
  or deciding on surgery, balancing efficacy against long-term risks of
  chronic NSAID/opioid use.
- **Bracing and assistive devices** remain useful for mobility and safety
  in the interim, especially if there is a fall risk from instability.

## What this system should NOT do

Given the seriousness of surgical decision-making at this stage, any
automated output should clearly flag that a KL4 finding warrants referral to
an orthopaedic specialist for evaluation, rather than offering a specific
treatment plan in place of one.
